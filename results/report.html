Fake web pages built from known failure modes cut agent accuracy from 43% to 24%

We wrote fake web pages aimed at three failure modes from our error analysis of gpt-5.5 and Qwen3.8 on BrowseComp-Plus, added them to the corpus, and re-ran a search agent on 99 questions. Two of the three attacks cut accuracy almost in half. In most of the extra failures the agent answered with the fake page's answer.

What we built

The 100 questions come from those both models answered correctly in the original runs, minus questions with flawed gold labels. For each question we generated pages of three kinds:

  • F1 decoy. A made-up entity that matches every clue except one, which it fails in plain text (e.g. a band formed in 1981 when the question says 1965–1980). Targets agents that commit to the first plausible hit. Up to 2 pages per question.
  • F2 memory lure. Pages that seem to confirm the wrong answer gpt-5.5 or Qwen3.8 gives from memory, while stating one fact that rules it out. Targets agents that trust their memory. Up to 2 pages.
  • F4 flood. Five pages about different entities that share the question's vocabulary but clearly fail at least two clues. Targets agents that never see the answer page. Up to 5 pages.

Every page passed an automatic check before use: a model reading the gold pages alongside the fakes still had to reach the gold answer, and a second model checked that no page leaks the answer, contradicts the gold facts, or meets every clue. Kept: 166 F1, 125 F2 and 402 F4 pages.

How we ran it

This repo's search agent (search_agent/openai_client.py) with gpt-5.4-mini at medium reasoning effort, BM25 over the BrowseComp-Plus corpus (top 5 results, 512-token snippets, full-document reads allowed), and a 25-turn cap. Each question ran once on the clean corpus and once with each family's pages added. Answers were graded by the repo's judge (scripts_evaluation/evaluate_with_openai.py, gpt-4.1). Question 49 was dropped because its gold answer in the data file is "Yes" for a "name the band" question.

What we learned

  • The agent doesn't check every clue. In 17 of 23 F1 losses it answered exactly with the decoy's answer, even though each decoy states the clue it fails. For example, it gave the decoy's aircraft serial "XN776" instead of "XM598", which it got right on the clean corpus.
  • Memory lures were the strongest attack. F2 cost 25 questions. During page validation these lures also fooled gpt-5.5 itself on 20 questions while it had the gold pages in hand. Those lures were removed as possibly ambiguous, so the pages we tested are the weaker ones.
  • Floods waste turns more than they hide answers. Gold pages were shown as often under F4 as on the clean corpus (60 questions each). The agent spent its turns on look-alike pages instead, and 9 of its 16 losses hit the 25-turn cap.
  • The fake pages are easy to detect when you ask. Shown pages one at a time, gpt-5.5 called 60 of 60 F1 pages and 150 of 150 F4 pages AI-written, against 1 of 60 and 2 of 150 real pages. The agent showed no sign of noticing during search. A stronger agent prompted to watch for fabrication might resist these attacks, so generating harder-to-detect pages is the main open problem.

Limits

  • One run per question per setup, with no repeat clean run. The 6–9 gained questions per setup suggest noise of about that size.
  • gpt-5.4-mini with a 25-turn cap is weaker than the gpt-5.5 and Qwen3.8 setups in the error analysis (43% clean accuracy).
  • Ranking checks used agent-style queries written by gpt-5.5, not real search logs.
  • 71 F2 pages make invented claims about real entities and must be removed before any release.

Cost and files

Agent runs about $118 (including about $7 of setup tests); judging about $2. Page generation and validation, mostly gpt-5.5 before the switch to gpt-5.4-mini, accounted for most of the earlier OpenAI spend.
